Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization

Por Zhepei Wei, Wenlin Yao, Baolin Peng, Danqing Wang e mais 4 · Microsoft Research

Self-HPO is a recursive framework that alternates harness and policy optimization for coding agents, improving performance by targeting evolving failure modes through reinforcement learning.

Large language model (LLM) agents have shown promising potential in coding tasks, and their performance is jointly determined by the policy model and the harness that mediates their interaction with the environment. Indeed, harness and policy are mutually reinforcing and evolving only one component is suboptimal---a harness tuned for one checkpoint may not target the failure modes of an updated policy, while policy learning remains limited to the trajectories elicited by a fixed harness. In light of this, we propose Self-HPO, a recursive self-improving framework for coding agents that alternates harness and policy optimization, using the evolving policy as its own harness proposer. Specifically, in each round, the current policy first analyzes its own failures and proposes new harness candidates (H-step). The policy is then updated through reinforcement learning (RL) under the selected harness (P-step), and hard tasks identified during training form the next harness search set, keeping harness optimization targeted to the policy's evolving failure modes. Across three coding benchmarks, Self-HPO consistently outperforms harness-only and policy-only baselines, improving Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate from 57.8% to 64.8%, with gains generalizing to out-of-distribution settings. Further analysis shows that both optimized components retain gains even when paired with their base counterparts, while their joint gain exceeds the sum of their individual gains. Self-HPO also improves reliability (Pass@1) and expands the capability frontier (Pass@k) over the base agent. Additionally, we show that with a stronger external model (e.g., Opus 4.8) as the harness proposer, the same framework yields a further performance boost.

SWE-Residual: Evaluating Starting-State Robustness of Coding Agents

Por Jinze Li, Weijie Liu, Jinfeng Xu e mais 6

Software engineering (SWE) benchmarks are the primary way to measure the capabilities of coding agents: an agent is placed in a code repository or machine environment, given a request, and judged by hidden tests. We introduce SWE-Residual, a benchmark for Starting-State Robustness: the ability to maintain performance on a task when the starting state already contains earlier work on that task. On DeepSWE, in 58.9--88.2% of failed runs, they submit with exactly the same tests failing, although they pass most of these tests from a Clean Start.

How Effectively Do Security Skills Guide Coding Agents in Vulnerability Detection in Linux Kernel?

Por Haoran Xue, Nikta Akbarpour, Gias Uddin e mais 1

Large language model (LLM) coding agents, such as Claude Code, are increasingly used for security auditing, with task-specific skills providing reusable vulnerability-analysis procedures. We conduct the first large-scale empirical study of 37 security skills using Claude Code with four underlying LLMs on 100 real Linux kernel vulnerabilities across ten classes, totaling 14800 runs. SKILL-SIF improves fully correct vulnerability detection by up to eight times (from 2 percent to 16 percent) on unseen vulnerabilities, while aggregate accuracy across four LLMs increases by 1.85 times (from 6.05 percent to 11.20 percent).

From Software History to Executable Regression Tasks: A System for Evaluating Software Agents

Por Ruikai Shi, Zhengran Zeng, Rui Xie e mais 2

As software agents increasingly generate and modify code, evaluating their ability to review changes and discover defects becomes important. We present a system that constructs executable regression instances from historical introducing--fixing commit pairs and developer tests. Precise root-line localization remains challenging, while baseline combinations achieve repair and regression-test generation success rates of up to 68.7% and 77.1%, respectively.

Noise-Awareness in Quantum Software Testing: An Empirical Landscape Analysis

Por Mir Mohammad Yousuf, Shabir Ahmad Sofi e Bisma Majid

This paper presents a systematic empirical landscape analysis of noise-awareness in QST research through a deterministic, taxonomy-guided analytical framework applied to 61 papers published between 2018 and 2025 across eight testing paradigms, contrasting how contributions are framed with how they are evaluated. Among the analyzed papers, 47 (77.0%) are noise-oblivious and only 14 (23.0%) explicitly engage with noise-related execution concerns, while only 11 (18.0%) report evaluation on physical hardware or a noisy simulator.

Safety Analysis of CPSs Over-the-air Updates: Test Case Prioritization and Selection

Por Andrea Bombarda, Luca Carminati, Nunzio Marco Bisceglia e mais 5

Over-the-air (OTA) updates for Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) allow engineers to modify their software wirelessly. To overcome this limitation, we propose CRISP (CRIticality-based Selection and Prioritization), a novel framework supporting test case prioritization and selection in the context of OTA updates. Our results show that CRISP outperforms our baselines in finding failure-revealing test cases, and that our selection procedure can be configured to find reasonable and practical tradeoffs among accuracy (percentage of failure-revealing test cases identified) and computational time (time required to find them).
