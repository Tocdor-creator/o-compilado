PyRUA-DataFarm: LLM Agent as a Cost-Efficient Teleoperator for Real-Ready Robot Data

Por Kewei Zhang, Juncheng Ma, Guodong Lv, Duomin Wang e mais 10 · DAGroup-PKU

A coding agent automates robot demonstration generation by simulating tasks, rendering photorealistic videos, and producing deployable training data at lower cost than teleoperation.

Teleoperation remains the default way to collect robot demonstrations, but every episode costs robot and operator time, and every new task starts from scratch. We ask whether a coding agent can take over the teleoperator's job. In our DataFarm, the agent receives a data request and one calibration episode of the robot in the target scene and rebuilds the task in simulation: it writes the scene, an expert controller, and a success judge. The simulator supplies exact actions, and a video model conditioned on real images renders each rollout as photographic video, generating all camera views together consistently. The output is real-ready data: synchronized camera views labelled with the robot's actions, on which a policy can be trained directly and deployed on the real robot. Independent judges certify the expert and every rendered episode before admission. On five real-robot tasks, policies trained only on farm data match policies trained on as many teleoperated episodes (93 vs. 84 successes in 100 trials), and under an unseen tablecloth and coloured light they keep 87 successes where the teleoperation-trained policies fall to 46. Adapting a task is fully automatic and costs \21 of API usage on average, paid once per task, plus 5 for a render contract; every further episode costs \0.80 on average for simulation, rendering, and review, with no robot or operator. One hour of three-view data for a task costs 107 on average, these costs included, slightly below a quote of \117 for teleoperation in China and below 290 in labour alone in North America.

Trace2Eval: Contextualized Evaluation of Coding Agents from Real-World Traces

Por Ji Shi, Lirong Jie, Lexiao Zou e mais 5

Coding agents are increasingly used in real-world workflows across diverse domains, motivating evaluation grounded in real user tasks. We propose Trace2Eval, a dynamically updatable pipeline that constructs benchmarks from real-world interaction trajectories. Guided by feedback from the Trace2Eval pipeline, models revise their initial artifacts, yielding a mean score gain of 10.05 percentage points across 19 model-harness configurations and a maximum of 19.28 percentage points for DeepSeek-V4-Pro with Claude Code, which supports rubric feedback as actionable guidance for artifact improvement.

EvoCodeAgent: Project-Level Code Generation via Architecture-Code Co-Evolution and Experience-Guided Repair

Por Qianhui Zhao, Li Zhang e Fang Liu

Project-level code generation aims to automatically synthesize complete software repositories from high-level requirements, producing coherent multi-file systems with well-structured modules and consistent cross-file dependencies. To address these challenges, we propose EvoCodeAgent, a state-centric framework for project-level code generation. Under the GPT-5 setting, it achieves unit test pass rates of 60.48% and 27.73%, respectively, yielding up to a 53.5% improvement over the strongest baseline.

Scythe: Semantic-Aware Rewriting for Fast,Effective Program Reduction

Por Ioanna Moraiti, Charalambos Mitropoulos, Artem Anchikov e mais 3

Automated program reduction shrinks a program into a smaller one that preserves a property of interest. We present SCYTHE, a fast, semantic-aware pre-processing step that runs in front of any grammar-based reducer without modifying it. SCYTHE also boosts LATRA, a reducer that, unlike the purely syntactic PERSES, also applies language-specific transformations: the combination produces programs 10.6% smaller (up to 61.8%) and is 2.4$\times$ faster on average.

TauLang: A Language and Compiler for Space-Time Programming on Statically Scheduled Accelerators

Por Xuxiao Yang, Junchi Wu, Feiyang Wu e mais 8

Statically scheduled accelerators such as Groq's Tensor Streaming Processor (TSP) achieve low-latency large language model inference by stripping dynamic synchronization hardware from the chip. We present TauLang, a kernel language and compiler that makes time a first-class citizen of the program. On a cycleaccurate model of a TSP-like machine, joint search achieves a $1.40\times$ latency speedup across ten kernels over a space-first flow.
