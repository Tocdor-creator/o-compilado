PrismaCoder: Pushing the Boundaries of Open Multimodal Code Intelligence through Decomposed Rubric Synthesis

Por Qiushi Sun, Jingyang Gong, Yang Liu, Qiaosheng Chen e mais 13 · NLP Group of The University of Hong Kong

PrismaForge uses a rubric-based pipeline to synthesize and filter high-quality multimodal code data, enabling open models that rival proprietary systems on visual coding tasks.

Multimodal code intelligence connects the symbolic world of programs with the visual world their execution creates. Interest in this capability is burgeoning, as models are increasingly asked to produce charts, webpages, animations, and semi-structured documents. Recently, proprietary models have pushed far ahead, producing interactive artifacts even from a text description or a screenshot alone. Yet their data and training remain closed, and open progress is held back by the quantity and quality of available data and by the pipelines and methods for producing it. Quality is the harder of the two, since a sample must be correct as a program, well built, and above all visually right once rendered. No existing signal covers all three. Execution often returns just a pass or fail, code similarity does not map to the visual output, and task-specific verifiers do not generalize. To push open progress further, we start from the data side with PrismaForge, a hierarchical data pipeline centered on a synthesized rubric that decomposes these factors into checkable criteria. The same rubric serves as both filter and reward: it curates the supervised corpus, then drives reinforcement learning on visual-coding tasks. Leveraging PrismaForge for data synthesis and filtering, we build PrismaCoder-2M, the most diverse open dataset for multimodal code intelligence and the largest to date. On the model side, we train PrismaCoder-9B and PrismaCoder-35B on this data and release both. They lead the open state of the art by a wide margin and rival leading proprietary systems. Our analyses further show that the proposed rubric-based pipeline improves multimodal coding performance across model backbones. We release our code and data at https://qiushisun.github.io/PrismaCoder-Home/.

AgenticRoboRL: Bringing Coding-Agent Post-Training to Agentic Robotics

Por Xinxin Liu, Bingxin Xu, Wenrui Bao e mais 4 · University of Central Florida

AgenticRoboRL improves robot planners via supervised fine-tuning and multi-turn reinforcement learning across diverse harnesses, significantly boosting success rates without external memory.

Agentic robotics systems combine planners with robot harnesses to perform complex manipulation tasks. To this end, we introduce AgenticRoboRL, a framework for post-training robot planners across different harnesses. On LIBERO-PRO, AgenticRoboRL raises the success rate of a 9B-parameter planner from 18.1% to 71.0% without external memory, approaching the 72.1% achieved by Codex (GPT-5.5) with external memory.

From RL Papers to Framework Code: Inclusion and Review in Seven Open-Source Training Frameworks

Por Taiheng Pan

Most proposed reinforcement learning training methods for language models are not adopted by open-source frameworks, with adoption rates remaining low despite growing publication volumes.

Practitioners use reinforcement learning methods for language models through training frameworks, so a method that a framework includes is one configuration change away for its users. We release the data as a tracker that links training failures to framework implementations and lists each framework's contribution rules and earlier proposals.

Por Yanze Zhang, Maggie Yongqi Guan, Shudong Liu e mais 4

Coding agents are becoming an increasingly important paradigm for automating software engineering tasks. To study this capability, we introduce MicroPerf-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating coding agents on end-to-end performance optimization in microservice systems. Across 1,755 runs covering nine agent-model configurations, the best configuration recovers only 42.0% of the injected performance degradation.

Sign Here: Attributing a Source for a More Trustworthy Software Supply Chain

Por Derek Garcia

Software supply chain attacks continue to be on the rise, exploiting the trust that helps open source ecosystems thrive. In this paper, we investigate whether removing the registry from the developer workflow by building packages directly from source can serve as a malware prevention strategy - effectively removing an entire attack surface. We evaluate this through three research questions: how effective is building from source at avoiding malware, how feasible is it to build from source in the current state of the supply chain, and how the building from source strategy compares to state-of-the-art security scanners.

On the Effectiveness of Snapshot Testing: An Empirical Study

Por Shun Fujita, Satoru Tomizawa, Yutaro Kashiwa e mais 2

Software testing is essential for ensuring quality, yet testing complex front-end user interfaces is often time-consuming and difficult. To fill this gap, this study presents a large-scale empirical investigation into the effectiveness of snapshot testing involving 340 GitHub repositories and a survey of 60 experienced practitioners. Our quantitative results demonstrate that combining snapshot and traditional non-snapshot tests significantly enhances code coverage, achieving a median statement coverage of 86.7%.

Reference Manual of MEDiml

Por Mahdi Ait Lhaj Loutfi, Simon Provost, \'Elodie Gillard e mais 13

Radiomics is the high-throughput extraction of quantitative features (e.g., tumor shape, intensity distributions, and texture) from medical images to characterize tissues and uncover disease patterns invisible to the human eye. Hence, we propose MEDiml, an open-source, standards-compliant radiomics platform connecting feature extraction, predictive modeling, and interpretation through complementary graphical and programmatic interfaces. Deciding Scale Placement in MXFP4 Dot Products with Integer Arithmetic

Por Shreyas Vinayakumar

The Open Compute Project's microscaling (MX) family includes MXFP4, which stores 32 four-bit elements with a shared power-of-two scale. We study the legality of compiler rewrites in either direction by deriving conditions under which the two formulations are bitwise equivalent. For MXFP4, we derive a closed-form sufficient upper bound on the sum of scale exponents for any fixed binary reduction tree within the supported coefficient range.
