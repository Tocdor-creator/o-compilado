In Situ Training of Optical Neural Networks by Coding Agents

Por Ilker Oguz, Colin Christian, Christophe Moser, Demetri Psaltis e mais 1 · Stanford University

A coding agent trains an optical neural network from measurements alone by reparametrizing it with interpretable optical elements, achieving better classification and image encoding than gradient-free methods and approaching backpropagation through a calibrated model at lower cost.

Optical neural networks promise fast, energy-efficient computing, but every device departs from its design and must be trained with the hardware. In situ training relies on an expert-built calibrated digital model of each device or on gradient-free trial and error. Coding agents, large language models that write and run programs, have shown an emergent ability to understand and edit the weights of digital neural networks. Here we introduce optics as code, a method in which a coding agent trains an optical neural network from measurements alone, through a harness that returns camera images of its probes and requires a hypothesis per request. We discovered that the agent forms a physical understanding of the optics and reparametrizes the network with interpretable optical elements tuned on the hardware. It outperforms gradient-free methods in classification and image encoding, approaching backpropagation through a calibrated model at a fraction of the cost. Needing neither per-device calibration nor gradients through the hardware, and improving with more capable models, optics as code offers a practical route to large, scalable optical neural networks and a general tool for calibrating and optimizing optical experiments.

SWE-Recreation: Benchmarking Coding Agents for Software Recreation

Por Zhiyao Xu, Zhanjie Ding, Hongyao Yu e mais 1

Agents increasingly operate software and even write it, yet they are almost always told what to achieve: given a goal, a mock-up, or a demonstration. We present SWE-Recreation, a benchmark of 54 real applications (28 websites and 26 Android apps) with 1,446 hidden requirements describing multistep, stateful workflows and explicit exclusions for behavior outside the reconstruction scope. Across 54 evaluated applications, five frontier systems reach at most 0.530 weighted fidelity and 22.9% fully reproduced requirements under application-equal averaging.

Behavioral Containment: Source-to-Binary Function Matching for 1-Day Vulnerability Detection

Por Yifei Yang, Xuejiao Luo e Penghui Li

Vulnerabilities in reused open-source code often propagate into deployed binaries. Building on this, we present SBIND, which decompiles the binary and checks behavioral containment instead of comparing code. On stripped binaries built with two compilers, two optimization levels, and four architectures, SBIND ranks the correct function first in 77.87% of 32,008 searches, against 45.69% for the state-of-the-art approach SBridge.

Tenon: A Unified Programming Framework for Retargetable PIM Compilation

Por Niansong Zhang, Shiran Guo e Zhiru Zhang

Processing-in-memory (PIM) is emerging as a building block of heterogeneous systems for memory-bound workloads, and commercial prototypes now span near-bank SIMD engines, bank-level GDDR accelerators, programmable DRAM cores, and bit-level in-SRAM processors. We present Tenon, a unified programming framework for retargetable PIM compilation. Across the commercial backends, it matches or outperforms vendor, expert, and compiler baselines, with geometric-mean speedups of up to $1.20\times$ and individual speedups up to $3.65\times$.

Mode Inference for Substructural Adjoint Types

Por Sophia Roshal e Frank Pfenning

Adjoint types [Jang et al. 2024] provide a method of mixing substructural modes (for example, linear, affine, strict, or unrestricted) in a type system while preserving the proof-theoretic and computational properties of each mode. In this paper we define a mode inference algorithm for programs with adjoint types that produces principal modes, summarizing all possible substructural modes of a function in a simple constraint language. We show that our system is sound and complete with respect to a mild restriction of Jang et al.'s monomorphic version.
