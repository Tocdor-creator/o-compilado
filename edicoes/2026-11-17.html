SWE-CoWork: Benchmarking Coding Agents as Digital Employees in Organizations

Por Xinping Lei, Minghao Liu, Ruijie Zhu, Xiaohui Hu e mais 22 · Tokenwave.AI

A benchmark of realistic software engineering assignments reveals that current coding agents struggle to track requirements from clarification through implementation, verification, and handoff, completing few tasks despite partial test success.

A coding agent working within a software organization must establish what to build, collaborate with colleagues, and verify what it delivers. We introduce SWE-CoWork, a benchmark comprising 101 development assignments across 101 real repositories, 11 programming languages, and 12 application domains. Each assignment places an agent in a simulated engineering organization with 26 colleagues who have distinct responsibilities, bounded knowledge, and controlled collaboration behavior. Starting from a brief handoff, agents must recover requirements distributed across colleagues and workplace records, reconcile conflicting evidence, and implement changes evaluated by private executable tests. We evaluate eleven models and find a substantial gap between partial implementation and complete task delivery. The two leading models satisfy approximately 63% of fail-to-pass (F2P) tests but complete fewer than 8% of assignments. Providing complete requirements upfront improves F2P by approximately 16 percentage points for Seed Evolving and GPT-6 Astra, yet leaves complete task success unchanged. Requirement-level trajectory analysis reveals failures throughout the delivery process: Claude-Opus-5.5 directs a mean of 93% of first questions about queried cards to their accountable owners across runs but obtains only 64% of colleague-held requirements. Deferred questions go unfollowed, implementations remain incomplete even after requirements are acquired, and 31% of requirements reported as completed fail their tests. These findings highlight sustained requirement tracking, from clarification through implementation, verification, and handoff, as a key challenge for reliable autonomous software engineering.

Accelerate Bench: Evaluating Agents for Application-Level Program Optimization

Por Bowen Liu, Yuhan Li, Nuo Chen e mais 7 · Tencent Hunyuan

Application optimization requires coding agents to discover useful changes and coordinate them across a complete program while preserving the required computation. We introduce Accelerate Bench, a benchmark of 32 CPU optimization tasks adapted from scientific applications and supercomputing competitions. Most of their programs are correct, and the size of the speedup separates them: the two leading agents are at least 2x faster on 22 or more of the 32 tasks, while the other fourteen reach 2x on 8 to 17 and leave 125 of their 412 correct outcomes below 1.25x.

Termination Lag: Coding Agents Solve Early but Stop Late

Por Musa Cim, Burak Topcu e Mahmut Taylan Kandemir · Pennsylvania State University

Coding agents often find correct patches early but continue working, sometimes undoing correct solutions, with termination lag varying by model and benchmark.

Coding agents can solve a task well before they stop working on it. We characterize termination lag--the number of steps between an agent's first correct patch and the end of its run--across five models, two agent frameworks, and three SWE-bench datasets by recording and testing every distinct repository patch generated during execution. Our analysis reveals that 38--82% of successful runs achieve a correct solution on their initial source-code edit.

Por Adri\'an Mar\'in Boyero, Fynn Otto, Albert Rico e mais 2
