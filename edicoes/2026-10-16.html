FDE-Gym: The Last Mile of AI Delivery

Por Dongxu Yang, Hongda Yan, Renxin Wang, Baoli Zhang e mais 22 · DeepLethe

FDE-Gym evaluates AI agents on realistic customer delivery tasks requiring clarification of ambiguous requirements and discovery of unstated constraints, revealing a substantial performance gap compared to well-specified benchmarks.

Existing agent benchmarks typically evaluate agents on well-specified tasks defined by issue reports, specifications, or task tickets. Real-world customer delivery is different: forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) must clarify ambiguous requirements, work in live systems, and uncover unstated constraints. We introduce FDE-Gym, a benchmark of 95 end-to-end delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors, each adapted by industry experts from real-world delivery projects. In simulated customer environments with scripted stakeholders and modifiable customer systems, agents interact with stakeholders and operate on production-like systems to deliver working solutions. FDE-Gym scores a delivery by how much it improves the customer's own success metric, normalized between 0 (unchanged system) and 1 (reference solution), with penalties for stakeholder interactions (messages, trial runs, and waiting time). An online platform lets humans practice FDE skills under the same grading criteria. We evaluate 13 model configurations from seven providers. In the realistic setting, where unstated constraints must be discovered, the best configuration achieves a mean FDE-Gym score of 0.56 and resolves only 14 of the 95 tasks (a score of at least 0.8 with no incident). Discovering unstated constraints is the primary bottleneck: performance drops sharply when models must uncover them without explicit guidance. Yet information discovery alone does not account for the gap: even on the 23 tasks where the best configuration obtains every key fact from stakeholders, it reaches a normalized business uplift of 0.8 or above on only 10. These results reveal a substantial gap between solving well-specified tasks and delivering effective AI systems for real customers.

AesCode: Aesthetic Code Generation with Decoupled Cross-Modal Rewards

Por Xin Gao, Yifan Yang, Xin Li e mais 13 · Microsoft

AesCode improves code generation for visual artifacts by combining design graphs with cross-modal rewards guided by reference images, enhancing layout, aesthetics, and verifiable correctness.

Generating information-rich visual artifacts such as slides, posters, and dashboards as code is appealing because the outputs are structured, editable, and verifiable. To evaluate this setting, we build a benchmark that scores verifiable correctness and visual quality separately, with visual scores highly consistent with human judgments. We find that reference images substantially improve the visual quality of code generated by existing models, and ablations show that AesCode benefits from references both as model input and as a source of visual rewards.

Doc2Harness: Benchmarking LLMs on Long-Horizon Harness Engineering of Coding Agents

Por Yuyao Ge, Shenghua Liu, Peizhong Gao e mais 8 · Institute of Computing Technology

Doc2Harness is a deterministic two-stage benchmark that evaluates long-horizon harness engineering by having models build and run harnesses across isolated containers, revealing substantial performance gaps among frontier models.

Agents driven by large language models are increasingly deployed on long-horizon tasks, where they use tools and modify real workspaces. We propose Doc2Harness, a two-stage benchmark that evaluates long-horizon harness engineering directly. Across nineteen frontier models, each evaluated with three independent builds on 328 test cases, GPT-6 Astra achieves the highest pass rate at 53.05%, while the weakest model reaches 27.44%, a gap of roughly twenty-six percentage points.

SWE-at-Scale: Scalable Issue Synthesis from Historical Bug Fixes

Por Silin Chen, Zihan Zhang, Xiaodong Gu e mais 7 · Shanghai Jiao Tong University

SWE-at-Scale synthesizes realistic software engineering tasks by inverting real-world fixes to generate diverse, executable bugs for evaluating coding agents.

As coding agents become increasingly capable, robust evaluation requires a broader and more diverse supply of verifiable software engineering (SWE) tasks. To exponentially scale SWE task production, we introduce SWE-at-Scale, a framework that automatically synthesizes realistic issues by reverse-engineering real-world software fixes. Extensive experiments show that SWE-at-Scale generates more diverse and realistic SWE tasks than current synthesis baselines while posing greater challenges to state-of-the-art coding agents.
